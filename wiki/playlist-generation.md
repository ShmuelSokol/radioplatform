# Playlist generation (rotation by song type)

Models the station's legacy program (KBR Studio "Automatic playlist generation"): a playlist is a repeating sequence of song types, started by a schedule rule, generated N hours ahead.

Mapping, no new tables:
- song type = `Asset.category` (with `Asset.asset_type`)
- playlist sequence = `PlaylistTemplate` + ordered `TemplateSlot`s
- start rule = the `ScheduleBlock` that names the template

`backend/app/services/playlist_generator_service.py::generate` walks forward from the end of the queue. For each moment it asks `SchedulingService.rank_matching_blocks` which block is on, takes the next slot in that block's template (cursor per block, saved in `station.automation_config.playlist_generation.cursor`) and draws a song:

1. Artist rule first: no artist within `artist_distance` songs. If nothing is clear, the rule gives way and the pick is flagged `artist_rule_relaxed`.
2. Longest-rested first (last `PlayLog.start_utc` on this station; never aired = most rested). Songs already waiting in the queue sort last.
3. Random among the most-rested third, but only songs that have rested at least half as long as the most rested one. Without that limit a song aired an hour ago was drawn ahead of one never aired.

Blackouts (Shabbos, Yom Tov) generate nothing and are reported in `Plan.notes`. Time with no block plays any music (pick `block_name` "No block"), so the station never goes quiet between blocks.

`automation_config.playlist_generation = {enabled, hours, artist_distance}`; when on, `QueueReplenishService` fills from it instead of the rule-based fill. It is also on, whatever the switch says, for any station whose active schedule has a block with an active playlist (`schedule_refresh_service.uses_block_playlists`); the first refill then throws away a week of old rule-based fill (more than 2 x `hours` of automatic songs).

## Schedule changes take over on time

Before 2026-11-16 the rule-based fill queued about 7 days ahead, so a new block waited behind ~1,450 old songs. Now every change to a schedule, block, playlist or playlist step calls `schedule_refresh_service.refresh_station`: pending `source="auto"` songs with no `preempt_at` are deleted and the queue is generated again from now. Songs a person queued, timed items and program-block entries stay. Only for stations that fill from blocks now or did before the change (`had_playlists`). The refresh calls `db.expire_all()` first: the route session just made the change and its cached playlist steps were stale (a deleted step was still drawn). Test: `tests/test_schedule_refresh.py`.

Generation starts where the queue really ends on the air (`QueueReplenishService._air_timeline`): the song on the air until its crossfade point, each waiting item for `on_air_seconds` (duration minus the crossfade, the same rule as `queue.switch_time`, default 3 s), and timed items where they fall (`Clock`: a hard one cuts the item on air at its time, a soft one goes on after it). The generator walks the same `Clock`, so each pick's expected start, and so its block, allows for crossfades, time checks and sponsor blocks. Counting full lengths put a block's first song early by about 3 s per song queued before it (live tests: 1.5 min, then 39 s early).

Even so, songs drawn hours ahead drift. `schedule_refresh_service.realign_before_block_change` runs on every refill (each song change): once per change, 15 minutes before the block-with-a-playlist on the air changes, it throws the automatic songs away and draws them again from the real time on air. `QueueEntry.block_id` records the block each automatic song was drawn for; `remove_automatic_songs` moves that block's cursor back by the songs removed, so the playlist carries on from the right step (used by schedule changes, regenerate and the redraw). A start-only ("runs until the next playlist starts") block holds until another start-only block starts: daily and weekly ones look back 8 days, a monthly one back to its previous day of the month (up to ~2 months, worked out directly, not day by day), and a one-time one with a date range holds until the range ends (before 2026-11-17 every kind dropped off after 8 days and plain music played; test `tests/test_monthly_running_playlist.py`). It counts only starts after its `created_at` (`SchedulingService.last_start`): before 2026-11-16 a block saved at 6:40 to start at 6:52 was "on" at once, because yesterday's 6:52 was in the past, and its playlist aired immediately. New queue entries are always placed after the item on air (position counters include `status="playing"`); the refill used to give the new first song the same position as the song on air. Test: `tests/test_block_waits_for_its_time.py`. Rule: an item belongs to the block on at its expected start, so the item that straddles a block's start comes from the previous block or filler. Test: `tests/test_block_on_time.py`.

## Songs added straight to a block

A block's own songs ("+ Add song" on /admin/schedules/:id/blocks, `PlaylistEntry` rows) were stored and never read until 2026-11-17: `SchedulingService.get_next_asset_for_block` has no caller. Now `generate` loads them (`block_songs`: enabled entries only, through `not_placeholder()`, so a placeholder or do_not_play entry is never drawn) and, while such a block is on, draws from them in the block's `playback_mode`: **sequential** walks them in position order with the block's cursor (so the redraw and `remove_automatic_songs` carry on from the right song), **shuffle** picks at random but leaves out the most recently used half (queued or aired), **weighted** draws by `weight`. The artist rule does not apply inside a block's own list (a one-song block repeats that song). **A block that names a working playlist keeps playing the playlist**; its own songs only take over a block with no playlist (so a block on air with stray songs listed does not change when this shipped). A block whose songs are all unfit falls back to plain music, so no silence. `uses_block_playlists` and the 15-minute redraw count blocks with songs, and adding, changing or removing an entry (or `set-prerecorded`) calls `refresh_station`, so the change is on the queue at once. Test: `tests/test_block_songs_reach_queue.py`.

API: `GET/PUT /playlists/generation`, `POST /playlists/generation/regenerate`.

Minimum vote: `Asset.vote` is a staff-set whole number (0 = unrated), edited on the song's page and shown in the library list beside "Last played". `playlist_generation.min_vote` (0 = off) keeps songs under that vote out of every draw. A type where NO song reaches the minimum (jingles, usually unrated) is drawn as if there were no minimum, and the preview says so; otherwise a minimum would silence the type. Listener ratings (`SongRating`) are a separate thing and do not feed this.

## Station IDs between songs

Shmuel, 2026-11-18: "a whole bunch of jingles ... you're listening to Kol B'ramah, WMDI 107.9 Lakewood ... sprinkle them around". Setting `automation_config.station_ids = {enabled, min_minutes (12), max_minutes (20), category ("station_id"), clear_minutes (3), start_hour (0), end_hour (24)}`, off by default, on the Stations page under Automation Config (hours are the station's own time; start after end runs past midnight; equal = all day). The logic is `StationIdSprinkler` (playlist_generator_service.py), used by both fills: the playlist fill (`generate`, Main, Jerusalem) and the rule fill (`QueueReplenishService._station_id_after` after each item `_fill_content` queues, All Day Stories; Shmuel 2026-11-18: IDs between stories too). An ID follows any programme item (song, story, shiur) but never a short one (`NOT_BEFORE_AN_ID`: jingle, spot, time, weather...). The spacing counts from the last ID waiting in the queue at its expected start (`queue_starts` from `_air_timeline`), so top-ups keep it.

The sprinkler adds one after an item once a random `min..max` minutes have passed since the last station ID of that category aired or was queued (`last_used` of the jingle pool, which already counts waiting ones), so a top-up keeps the spacing across the join. Not when the item before is a short one (`after_type` for the first pick: the last ordinary queue item), not outside the hours, not in a blackout, and not within `clear_minutes` of a timed item on either side (`Clock.clear_of_timed`; `Clock.last_timed_end` and `timed_ended` from `_air_timeline` cover a break just before the fill). If it cannot go it waits for the next song. The draw is the usual longest-rested draw from `jingle / <category>`, so every ID airs before one repeats. Picks have `block_id` "" and `block_name` "Station ID"; the queue rows are ordinary `source="auto"` rows, redrawn with the songs. `hour_fit` never moves a non-music row, so it cannot pull an ID up against the hour. Jingles are not crossfaded (the song ends, then the ID). KBR Jerusalem's IDs use category `station_id_jerusalem`. Scripts and beds: project files `station-ids/`. Tests: `tests/test_station_ids_between_songs.py`.

The older "Hourly Station ID" switch (`hourly_station_id`, category `hourly_id`, in the :00 group) is separate.

## Items no automatic pick may use

`fit_for_random_pick()` (models/asset.py) skips categories do_not_play/test (any case) and one-moment clips. Placeholders listed by title in `NEVER_AUTO_TITLES` (the old "KOL BRAMAH TEST SPONSOR", category "Spots"; also "Kol bramah sponsor" and "Kol bramah ad", confirmed placeholders by Shmuel 2026-11-16; exact title match, any case) are skipped by **every** automatic pick through `not_placeholder()`, including rotations, rules and program blocks that name the category. Each refill (and each restart) deletes pending copies an automatic pick queued; operator, request and loop entries stay. Test: `tests/test_placeholder_never_auto.py`.

Category **do_not_play** (any case, `NEVER_AUTO_CATEGORIES`) is in `not_placeholder()` too since 2026-11-17, so a rule, block or playlist that names it gets nothing. At pick time `_skip_placeholders` (queue.py) marks pending automatic entries of either kind `skipped`; the playout engine's `pick_next` runs the same guard (before that it did not, so an engine station could air a queued placeholder). Only an operator's own pick (manual, request, loop) still plays one. Test: `tests/test_do_not_play_and_codec.py`.

Uploads are checked by codec, not extension: `convert_audio` probes the first audio stream and re-encodes when a file named `.mp3` is not MP3 (160 old files were MP2 inside `.mp3`; Safari and the engine refuse those). `.mp2`/`.mpg` uploads now become MP3 instead of being kept as MP2.

Since 2026-11-17 (library tests): the upload form's "MP2" choice is stored as MP3 too (before, the choice won and MP2 was stored). A file FFmpeg cannot read (PDF, image, broken file) raises `UnreadableAudioError` in `convert_audio` and `/assets/upload` answers 415 with nothing stored; FFmpeg failing on a readable file is refused the same way (the original used to be stored under a success message). Only a machine with no FFmpeg at all keeps the old store-as-is fallback. Uploads over `MAX_UPLOAD_MB` (600) get 413 before the file is read; an empty file 422. Open owner question: "Keep Original" of an MP2 file and the OGG choice still store formats Safari or the engine may not play (strict xfail in `tests/test_library_assets.py`).

## Editing steps is instant

Adding, removing or moving a step changes the cached playlists before the server answers (`usePlaylists.ts`, optimistic with rollback on error). The server answers at once too: the queue rebuild after a playlist edit runs 1.5 s later in its own session (`_schedule_refresh` in `api/v1/playlists.py`), and edits within that window share one rebuild per station. Schedule, block and block-song saves (`api/v1/schedules.py`: block create/edit/delete, "+ Add song" add/edit/remove, set-prerecorded, schedule edit/delete) use the same background rebuild, so they answer at once too; the waiting rebuild remembers whether the station had block playlists or songs before any edit in the window. The rebuild swaps old songs for new in ONE transaction (`replace_automatic_songs` holds the refill's own commits; the 15-minute redraw before a block defers its commit the same way), so the player never sees a queue without songs; the next automatic song is kept (`KEEP_NEXT`) so a player moving on mid-redraw never waits on a deleted row. Tests set `REFRESH_INLINE = True` (conftest) to see the rebuilt queue in the response.

Repeats when the fill outlasts the songs (fixed 2026-11-18, after Jerusalem aired one song at 6:49 and 7:04 AM): the draw puts songs "already waiting in the queue" last, but treated all of them alike. With 48 hours queued from a type that lasts fewer hours, every song of the type is waiting, so the pick was random among them and a song could come back two songs later. And the top-up at each song change knew a waiting song only by when it last AIRED, so a never-aired song queued near the end looked like the most rested. Now `generate(queued=[...])` gets the queue in order and `_Library` counts each waiting song as used when it will air (`QUEUED_PLACE_SECONDS` a place back from where the new songs start), and waiting songs are ordered by that. Two copies of one song that were merged into one asset (duplicate cleanup, 2026-11-17) leave the same kind of pair in an already-filled queue: nothing repairs a filled queue by itself, so draw it again with `POST /playlists/generation/regenerate?station_id=` (keeps what is playing, the next song, timed items, sponsorships and anything a person queued). It now also works for a station that fills from its blocks while "Generate from sequences" is off (it answered 409). Tests: `test_playlist_generation.py::test_a_fill_longer_than_the_song_list_keeps_repeats_far_apart`, `test_admin_crud.py::test_regenerate_works_for_a_station_that_fills_from_its_blocks`.

The rule-based fill (stations with no playlists, such as All Day Stories) had the same gap in its own code (`QueueReplenishService._fill_content`, fixed 2026-11-18): once every item of the type is in the queue it drew "pass 3" in a fresh random order per round, so the end of one round met the start of the next (one story twice 11 minutes apart), and a one-item top-up could pick the item queued a moment ago. Now `_in_turn` yields the item whose last place in the queue (`last_place`) is furthest back, at random among the most rested quarter (a list is still used at most 10 times per fill, so one song never fills a week). `POST /playlists/generation/regenerate` no longer answers 409 for any station: it redraws whichever way the station fills. Tests: `tests/test_rule_fill_spacing.py`.

How long a redraw holds its changes (2026-11-18). A redraw (Regenerate, a schedule or block save, the redraw 15 minutes before a block's playlist takes over) used to delete the automatic songs and then pick the new ones inside one transaction, so the player never saw an empty queue. Picking was one statement per song then: about 800 on Main, 7 to 8 s on the live database, and longer on the first draw after a restart, which reads the whole play history (`last_played`). All that time the deleted rows were locked: the renumber after a song change, the playout engine's start report and a library delete (42 s once) waited behind them. Now `QueueReplenishService` only notes which songs go (`replacing`, from `schedule_refresh_service.songs_to_replace`, which reads), leaves them out of every count while picking (`_not_replaced`), and `_fill_from_playlists` ends its read transaction and then swaps old for new in one short transaction (`_delete_replaced` + inserts + the saved playlist position: 3 statements in the measurement, was 775). A song that went on air or that the engine took as its next track during the picking stays. If the picking fails nothing changes. A rule-filled station (All Day Stories) still deletes first with its commits held back; its redraw is only the manual Regenerate. Measure: local Postgres, statements and seconds between the first change and the commit (recipe in the test's docstring); tests `tests/test_redraw_lock_time.py` (statement count, so it does not depend on the machine).

How long the picking takes (2026-11-18). After the lock fix a Regenerate on Jerusalem still took 20.6 s, and a queue delete sent beside it 2.9 s. No lock: the picking is Python on the one thread that answers every request, so while it runs every other request waits for a turn between picks (start reports and Next too). `_Library.draw` sorted the whole type again for every pick (16 ms for 8,000 songs) and `on_air` asked the database for each drawn song's crossfade point. Now each type is sorted once into `orders` (cards `(waiting, last used, random tie-break, id, artist, row)`) and `mark` moves a drawn song to its new place in every type that holds it (bisect); the draw walks from the front and stops at the first song that is waiting or not rested enough. The pools carry `cross_start` and `analysis_version` (JSON fields read in the pool query; `on_air` applies the same trust rule as `stored_analysis`), a block's own songs still read theirs one by one. `generate` yields (`asyncio.sleep(0)`) before every pick, because nothing in the loop waits on the database any more. A redraw takes the station's refill lock (`_refill_lock` in `api/v1/queue.py`). Local Postgres, two types of 8,000 songs, 730 picks: 12.05 s and 776 statements before, 1.70 s and 47 after. The picking rules are unchanged; the only difference is that equally rested songs keep one random order for a fill instead of a new one per pick. Tests: `tests/test_playlist_generation.py` (crossfade point with the list, a song drawn for one type goes back in every type, other requests get turns), `tests/test_redraw_lock_time.py` (under 80 statements a redraw). The live log of that Regenerate also showed one 7.35 s query: the full read of the station's play history (`services/last_played.py`), its first in that process three minutes after a restart. It has its index (`ix_play_logs_station_asset_start`); the cost is grouping the whole history. No request waits for it now: `warm_all` reads every active station in the background at startup, and the hourly full re-read runs in the background in its own session (`_reload`) while requests take what is known plus the new plays (before, whichever request came first each hour paid for it, a song change's top-up included). Tests: `tests/test_last_played_cache.py`.

What an ordinary top-up costs (2026-11-18). After the picking fix a plain queue delete still took 2.2 s on the live site and the engine's start reports 3 to 4.5 s. Both ran the top-up inside the request, and a top-up was about 44 database round trips even to add one song: 0.1 s locally, seconds from Railway to Supabase. Three changes. The station is read once per session (`QueueReplenishService._station`, `db.get` in `program_block_service` and `spot_schedule_service`): it was read five times with its channels, ten round trips. A queue filled from playlists is only topped up once it is `TOP_UP_SLACK_SECONDS` (20 minutes, or a quarter of a short queue) below its length, so most song changes draw nothing (a redraw ignores the slack). `DELETE /stations/{id}/queue/{entry}` commits, answers and tops up afterwards with `_replenish_soon`. Local count: 44 round trips to 22. The start report's own top-up call is the engine thread's (`playout_engine.py`). Tests: `tests/test_top_up_cost.py`.

The play history read, second round (2026-11-18). After the warm-up fix a refill of All Day Stories at 09:00Z still waited 16.4 s for the whole-history read: its station had not been warmed after the restart, and the first asker reads. Now nothing in a request reads the whole history (`services/last_played.py`): the first read of a station is the last `FIRST_READ_DAYS` (30) as plain rows by start time, later reads are the plays since the last one, both without GROUP BY; the whole history comes from `_reload` in the background, one station at a time (`_reload_turn`), once after the first read and then daily. Until it lands (seconds after start) a song last aired more than 30 days ago counts as not aired, which only changes the order among the most rested songs. The same grouped read takes 0.14 s on a local 2.1-million-row table, so 16 s is the live database on a big cold read; the index it needs exists. Tests read the whole history inline (`BACKGROUND_FULL_READ = False` in conftest); `tests/test_last_played_cache.py` switches it on.

A failed whole-history read is tried again after ten minutes, and the startup read tries each station three times (the 08:35Z restart on 2026-11-18 lost every station to one dropped connection). When a whole-history read takes over 3 s the backend logs, once per process, a line starting `LAST-PLAYED DIAGNOSIS` with the table's indexes, sizes and the database's own EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) of the read: nobody has SQL access to the live database, and this is how to see whether `ix_play_logs_station_asset_start` exists there and is used. Every `SLOW QUERY` line now shows the first 500 and last 300 characters of the statement and the request it ran for (`for GET /api/v1/...`, or `for background`).

Where the seconds go (2026-11-18). The diagnosis line showed the database running the whole-history read in 129 ms while the app waited 12.0 s for it, so a `SLOW QUERY` line measures the app's wait, not the database. Three things can fill that wait, and the log now tells them apart: the one event loop being held by Python that does not `await` (`EVENT LOOP HELD for N s so far by: <the code>`, from `app/core/loop_watch.py`, and "loop held N s of it" on every `SLOW QUERY` and `SLOW CONNECT` line); a new database connection being opened inside the request (`SLOW CONNECT`, about 2 s each; the pool keeps 10 open now, not 5, since every request beyond five at once opened and threw away its own); and the pool being full ("pool N in use of 10" on the same lines). A refused connection is tried again for up to 8 s (`app/db/engine.py` `_connect`), and startup waits for the database to answer. `play_logs` and `queue_entries` are vacuumed two minutes after a start when no vacuum ran in the last week (index-only scans were fetching nearly every row from the table), and autovacuum is set to come at 2% growth for them.

The first read after a start. With the loop, pool and connection ruled out, the first whole-history read after each start still took 12 to 16.5 s (even for the smallest station) against 0.13 s a moment later, and a 48 MB `play_logs` took 27 s to vacuum: signs of a small database reading from disk. Two log lines settle it after every start: `LAST-PLAYED DIAGNOSIS` is now that first read itself, run under EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS), so its `read=` (disk) against `hit=` (memory) counts and its own execution time are the cold ones; and `DATABASE LINK` (45 s after a start) gives the time of one `SELECT 1` (best, typical, worst of seven), this server's region, the database host name, and the database's `shared_buffers`, `effective_cache_size`, `max_connections` and size. A request is about four statements' worth of round trips (BEGIN, parse, run, COMMIT), so the `SELECT 1` time times four is the floor for any one-query request.

What it was (2026-11-18, from those lines). Not distance: one `SELECT 1` takes 3 ms (Railway us-east4, Supabase close by; the app uses the direct database host, `max_connections` 60, which is why the pool is 10 + 4 and not larger: an old and a new process overlap during a deploy). Not size: `shared_buffers` 224 MB against a 258 MB database. It was `play_logs` never having been vacuumed: the index-only scan fetched 139,992 of 145,557 rows from the table. After the first VACUUM (27 s) it fetched 149 and the read took 29 ms. The vacuum job (`_vacuum_stale_tables` in `app/main.py`) covers `play_logs`, `queue_entries`, `assets` and `alerts`, runs when a table had no vacuum for a week or holds over 10% dead rows, and logs one line per table either way with live and dead rows and sizes ("Vacuumed ..." or "Vacuum not needed for ...").

The first use of a type on a connection. A lookup in the tiny `users` table took 0.6 to 0.95 s per request, and a new connection 1.7 to 2.7 s, while `SELECT 1` took 3 ms. JIT was suspected and refuted: the live database already has `jit` off, and a lone new connection's first typed query costs 23 ms (`FIRST USE OF A TYPE`, logged after each start beside `DATABASE LINK`). The cost appears only when many connections open at once, and the pool replaced every connection after 5 minutes; connections now live 30 minutes and the pool keeps 10 + 4, which took the slow lines from 140.9 s to 24.9 s per 15 minutes. Never pass `server_settings` or `-c` options: the Supabase pooler refuses unknown startup parameters ("unsupported startup parameter: jit" stopped every connection of the 11:14Z deploy on 2026-11-18; `tests/test_pooler_startup.py` fails on any), and a session `SET` does not stick in transaction mode.

Waiting and playing rows. `queue_entries` keeps every entry ever played (about 255k rows on 2026-11-18) and only about 1,850 wait. The next due timed item (`_pick_next_pending`, GET /now-playing), the timed-groups check and the row on air walked that history: 0.94 s, 3.6 s and 0.95 s, with the loop free and the pool nearly empty. Partial indexes `ix_qe_pending_preempt` (station, preempt_at, position WHERE status = 'pending') and `ix_qe_playing_started` (station, started_at WHERE status = 'playing') hold only those rows: 0.01 to 0.06 ms locally on 250k history rows. They are on the model and in `CONCURRENT_INDEXES`. Statements are unnamed (no statement cache), so Postgres plans each with its real `status` value and can use a partial index even though status is a bind parameter.
